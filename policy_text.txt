1. Purpose, Scope and Governance
Purpose. To state the mission of the College and explain how policies are made, interpreted and changed.
1.1 Mission and Values
Meridian Crest College, founded in 1962, is a residential and commuter college of arts, sciences, business
and health professions. Its mission is to provide rigorous, affordable and inclusive undergraduate education
that prepares graduates for careers, graduate study and civic life. The College values integrity, curiosity,
respect, accountability and service.
1.2 Scope and Authority
These policies apply to every member of the College community on campus, at College-sponsored events, in
online courses, and off campus when conduct affects the educational mission or the safety of others.
Authority rests with the Board of Trustees, which delegates day-to-day administration to the President. The
Provost oversees academic policy; the Vice President for Student Affairs oversees student life and conduct;
the Vice President for Finance and Administration oversees fiscal and operational policy.
1.3 Policy Development and Amendment
  New or revised policies are drafted by the responsible office, reviewed by the relevant governance
committee (Academic Senate, Student Affairs Council or Staff Council) and approved by the
President's Cabinet.
 Policies that affect academic standards, degree requirements or faculty rights require approval by the
Academic Senate.
 The College may issue interim policies when required by law, accreditation or safety; interim policies
expire after 180 days unless formally adopted.
 Changes are published on the College website with an effective date. Students are governed by the
degree requirements in the catalog year of their first enrollment; conduct, financial and safety policies
apply as currently published.
1.3.1 Interpretation and Severability
The Provost (academic matters) and the General Counsel (legal matters) are the final interpreters of this
handbook. If any provision is found invalid, the remaining provisions stay in effect.

2. Equal Opportunity and Nondiscrimination
Purpose. To ensure that educational programs, employment and services are open to all qualified people.
2.1 Policy Statement
The College does not discriminate on the basis of race, color, ethnicity, national origin, ancestry, religion,
sex, pregnancy, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, age, disability, veteran or military status,
genetic information, marital or family status, or any other legally protected characteristic in admissions,
financial aid, employment, housing, athletics or any other program.
2.2 Reasonable Accommodation and Religious Observance
  Qualified individuals with disabilities receive reasonable accommodations through Accessibility
Services (see Policy 14.3).
 Students may request excused absences for religious observances. Instructors must provide a
reasonable opportunity to make up missed work when notice is given at least two weeks in advance.
 Employees may request accommodation for disability or sincerely held religious practice through
Human Resources.
2.3 Reporting and Non-Retaliation
Complaints of discrimination or harassment may be made to the Office of Equity and Compliance, to any
Dean, or through the anonymous reporting line. Retaliation against anyone who reports in good faith or
participates in an investigation is prohibited and is itself a violation of policy.
3. Admissions
Purpose. To describe how applicants are evaluated and admitted in a fair and transparent way.
3.1 Admissions Philosophy
Admission is competitive and holistic. The College considers academic preparation, course rigor, grades,
essays, recommendations, extracurricular and community contributions, and personal circumstances.
Standardized tests are optional; submitted scores are used only to the applicant's advantage.
3.2 First-Year Applicants
  Completion of secondary school (or equivalent) with a minimum of 16 college-preparatory units,
including four in English, three in mathematics, three in laboratory or natural science, three in social
science and two in a world language.
 A completed application, official transcripts, one essay and one counselor or teacher recommendation.
 Application fee of the amount published in the Schedule of Fees; fee waivers are available for
demonstrated financial need.
 Priority deadline November 15 (Early Action, non-binding); Regular deadline February 1; rolling
admission for remaining places until July 1.

3.3 Transfer Applicants
  Applicants must have a cumulative GPA of at least 2.5 from regionally accredited institutions.
 Up to 60 credits may transfer from two-year colleges and up to 90 credits in total from all sources,
subject to the 30-credit residency requirement in Policy 7.2.
 Only courses with a grade of C (2.0) or better transfer; grades do not transfer into the College GPA.
 Articulation agreements with partner community colleges guarantee transfer of designated courses.
3.4 International Applicants
International applicants submit credential evaluations from an approved evaluator, proof of English
proficiency (for example TOEFL 80, IELTS 6.5 or Duolingo 105, or completion of the College's intensive
English pathway) and a statement of financial support. The College issues immigration documents only after
the enrollment deposit and financial certification are received.
3.5 Admission Decisions, Deposits and Deferrals
  Decisions are released on a published schedule. Admitted students confirm by May 1 with a
nonrefundable enrollment deposit; deposits for fall may be refunded on written request before May 1.
 Admission may be deferred for one academic year for military service, medical reasons, or approved
service and gap-year programs.
 Admission is conditional on final transcripts and on the accuracy of the application. Falsified or
omitted information may result in rescission of admission or dismissal.
3.6 Readmission
Students who have been away for two or more consecutive semesters must apply for readmission. Students
who left in good standing are generally readmitted under the current catalog. Students who left on
suspension or dismissal must meet the conditions set in their separation letter.
4. Registration and Enrollment
Purpose. To define how students enroll, adjust schedules, attend class and take leaves.
4.1 Academic Calendar and Credit Hour
The academic year has fall and spring semesters of 15 instructional weeks plus a final examination week,
and optional summer sessions. One credit hour represents at least one hour of direct instruction and two
hours of out-of-class work per week over a 15-week semester, or an equivalent amount of work in other
formats.

4.3 Registration, Add and Drop
  Students register through the student portal during published windows by class standing. Holds
(financial, health, conduct or advising) must be cleared before registering.
 Students may add courses through the end of the first week and drop courses without a record through
the end of the second week.
 Courses dropped during the second week will not appear on the transcript.
4.4 Withdrawal from a Course
From the start of week three through the end of week ten, a student may withdraw from a course and receive
a grade of W, which carries no grade points. After week ten, withdrawal is permitted only for documented
extenuating circumstances approved by the Dean of the student's college. Students may use no more than six
W grades toward their degree without Dean approval.
4.5 Class Attendance
Regular attendance is expected. Faculty set attendance requirements in the syllabus and must state how
absences affect grades. Instructors excuse absences for College-sponsored activities, military duty, jury
service, religious observance, documented illness, and bereavement, and must allow reasonable make-up
work. A student who does not attend during the first two weeks may be administratively dropped.
4.6 Leave of Absence and Withdrawal from the College
  A leave of absence of up to two consecutive semesters may be approved by the Registrar for medical,
military, family or other compelling reasons. The student keeps catalog rights during an approved
leave.
 A complete withdrawal is initiated through the Dean of Students. Refunds follow Policy 11.4 and
financial aid is adjusted under Policy 12.4.
 Students called to active military duty receive a full tuition refund or the option of an Incomplete or W
grade as the student prefers.

4.7 Course Repeat Policy
A student may repeat a course in which a grade below C was earned. The most recent grade replaces the
earlier grade in the GPA, but both appear on the transcript. A course may be attempted no more than three
times without permission from the Dean.

5.7 Grade Appeals
A student who believes a final grade is inaccurate or arbitrary must first discuss it with the instructor within
15 business days of the grade posting. If unresolved, the student may submit a written appeal to the
department chair within 10 business days, then to the Dean, whose decision is final except for procedural
irregularities, which may be reviewed by the Provost. Appeals must cite specific grounds: calculation error,
departure from the syllabus, or discriminatory or capricious evaluation.
5.8 Final Examinations
Final examinations are given during the scheduled examination week. A student with three examinations in
one day may ask to reschedule one. Instructors may not change the date or time of a final examination
without Dean approval. Accommodations for disability are arranged through Accessibility Services.

6. Academic Integrity
Purpose. To protect the value of College degrees by setting clear expectations of honest scholarship.
6.1 Standard
Students are responsible for submitting work that is their own and for acknowledging the contributions of
others. Ignorance of the standards is not a defense; students should ask instructors when uncertain.
6.2 Violations
  Plagiarism: presenting another person's words, ideas or data as one's own, including text, images or
code generated by a tool when not permitted or not disclosed.
 Cheating: using unauthorized materials, devices or assistance in any assessment.
 Fabrication and falsification: inventing data or sources, or altering records or documents.
 Multiple submission: submitting the same work for credit in more than one course without
permission.
 Facilitation: helping another student violate this policy, including selling or sharing course materials
in a way the instructor has prohibited.
 Interference: disrupting or damaging another student's work or access to library or lab resources.
6.3 Use of Generative AI Tools
Use of generative artificial intelligence tools is governed by each instructor's syllabus. Where no statement
is given, students must obtain permission before using such tools for graded work and must disclose any
permitted use. Using AI-generated output without disclosure is treated as plagiarism.

7.2 Residency
At least 30 of the final 36 credits and at least half of the credits in the major must be earned at the College.
Credits earned through Advanced Placement, International Baccalaureate and CLEP examinations may
satisfy requirements but count as transfer credit, not residency credit.
7.3 Catalog Rights and Substitutions
A student may complete requirements under the catalog in effect at first enrollment for up to eight years (six
for associate degrees), or opt in to a later catalog in its entirety. Course substitutions and waivers require
written approval of the department chair and the Registrar.
7.4 Applying for Graduation and Commencement
  Students apply for graduation by the deadline published each semester, normally the end of the third
week of the term of intended completion.
 Degrees are conferred in May, August and December. Commencement is held once each year in May;
students within six credits of completion and with a plan to finish by August may participate.
 Diplomas are withheld from students with unresolved financial or conduct holds.
7.5 Double Majors, Minors and Second Degrees
Students may complete two majors within one degree if each major's requirements are met and at least 24
credits are unique to each major. A second bachelor's degree requires at least 30 additional credits at the
College beyond the first degree.

8. Student Records and Privacy
Purpose. To protect the confidentiality and accuracy of education records.
8.1 Rights Under FERPA
Under the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA), students have the right to (a) inspect and
review their education records within 45 days of a request, (b) request amendment of inaccurate or
misleading records, (c) consent to disclosure of personally identifiable information except where FERPA
allows disclosure, and (d) file a complaint with the U.S. Department of Education.
8.2 Directory Information
The College may release directory information without consent: name, College email address, major, dates
of attendance, enrollment status, degrees and honors received, and participation in recognized activities and
athletics. Students may restrict release by submitting a form to the Registrar at any time.
8.3 Disclosure to Parents and Others
Information from education records is not shared with parents or guardians without the student's written
consent, except where permitted by law (for example, when the parent claims the student as a dependent for
tax purposes, in a health or safety emergency, or when the student under 21 has violated an alcohol or drug
policy). Students may grant access to a designated proxy through the portal.

9. Student Code of Conduct
Purpose. To maintain a safe, respectful learning community and define fair procedures when expectations
are not met.
9.1 Jurisdiction
The Code applies to conduct on College property, at College-sponsored or supervised activities, online when
using College systems, and off campus when conduct poses a threat or seriously affects the community.
Students are responsible for the conduct of their guests.
9.2 Prohibited Conduct
  Violence, threats, harassment, stalking, bullying or intimidation, including by electronic means.
 Hazing of any kind, regardless of consent (see Policy 18.3).
 Theft, vandalism, unauthorized entry or misuse of College property or identification.
 Possession of firearms, explosives or other weapons (Policy 15.4), or setting off false alarms or
tampering with fire safety equipment.
 Violation of alcohol, drug or smoking policies (Policy 15.3).
 Disruption of teaching, research, administration or College events; failure to comply with the lawful
directions of College officials.
 Providing false information to the College or knowingly filing a false report.
 Violation of any other published College policy, or of local, state or national law when the conduct also
affects the College community.

The Vice President for Student Affairs may place a student on interim suspension, restricting access to
campus, classes or housing, where there is a reasonable belief the student poses a continuing threat to people

business days to challenge the basis.
9.6 Amnesty and Medical Amnesty
A student who seeks emergency help for themselves or another person because of alcohol or drug use will
not be charged with a Code violation for use or possession arising from that incident, but may be required to
complete an educational intervention. Amnesty does not apply to violence, distribution or repeated
incidents.
10. Sexual Misconduct, Harassment and Title IX
Purpose. To prevent and respond promptly and equitably to sex-based discrimination and violence.
10.1 Prohibited Conduct
The College prohibits sex discrimination, sexual harassment, sexual assault, dating and domestic violence,
stalking and retaliation, as defined by Title IX of the Education Amendments of 1972, the Violence Against
Women Act amendments to the Clery Act and applicable state law. Consent must be knowing, voluntary and
affirmative; it cannot be given by someone who is incapacitated, asleep or coerced.
10.2 Title IX Coordinator and Reporting
The Title IX Coordinator, based in the Office of Equity and Compliance, oversees the College's response.
Anyone may report to the Coordinator, Campus Safety or the online form at any time. Confidential support
is available from the Counseling Center, the campus health service and the sexual assault advocate. Most
employees are responsible employees who must promptly report incidents they learn about to the
Coordinator.

10.3 Supportive Measures and Response
  On receiving a report, the Coordinator contacts the affected person within two business days to offer
supportive measures such as no-contact directives, changes in housing or class schedule, academic
extensions, counseling and safety planning, regardless of whether a formal complaint is filed.
 A formal complaint starts a grievance process with a prompt, impartial investigation; both parties may
have an advisor, review evidence and submit witness lists.
 A trained decision-maker (separate from the investigator and the Coordinator) decides responsibility
using the preponderance of the evidence after a live hearing or written questioning procedure, normally
within 90 days of the formal complaint, with extensions for good cause.
 Both parties may appeal on grounds of procedural irregularity, new evidence or conflict of interest.
Sanctions range from warning to expulsion for students and up to termination for employees.
10.4 Retaliation and Training
Retaliation is prohibited. All new students and employees complete prevention and awareness training
within 30 days of arrival, with annual refreshers for employees.
11. Tuition, Fees and Refunds
Purpose. To explain how charges are set, billed and collected, and how refunds are calculated.
11.1 Setting of Tuition and Fees
The Board of Trustees approves tuition, fees and room and board each February for the next academic year.
Current rates are published in the Schedule of Fees. Fees may include technology, student activity, health
service, laboratory, studio and course-specific fees. Tuition is guaranteed not to increase by more than the
percentage published by the Board for continuous full-time students.
11.2 Billing and Payment
  Statements are issued electronically about 45 days before each term. Payment is due by the published
due date (normally 14 days before classes start) unless the student has enrolled in a payment plan.
 The payment plan divides the net balance into up to four installments with an enrollment fee; accepted
forms of payment include bank transfer, cards (service fee applies) and checks.
 Pending financial aid that is verified and approved counts as payment.
11.3 Late Payment, Holds and Collections
Accounts not paid by the due date incur a late fee and may be placed on hold, which blocks registration,
grades release, transcripts and diplomas. Accounts more than 90 days past due may be referred to a
collection agency, and the student is responsible for reasonable collection costs. No student is dropped
solely because of an unpaid balance if a payment plan agreement is in good standing.

12. Financial Aid
Purpose. To describe eligibility for and administration of grants, scholarships, loans and student
employment.
12.1 Applying for Aid
Students apply each year using the national or state financial aid application, plus any institutional form
requested. Priority date is March 1 for continuing students and the application deadline for new students.
Awards depend on demonstrated need, merit criteria or program funds, and are contingent on enrollment and
verification of the information supplied.
12.2 Types of Aid
  Grants (federal, state, institutional): need-based, do not require repayment.
 Merit scholarships: awarded at admission for academic excellence; renewable with a minimum
cumulative GPA (normally 3.0) and full-time enrollment, for up to eight semesters.
 Work-study and campus employment: up to 20 hours a week while classes are in session.
 Loans: federal and private education loans; students are required to complete entrance counseling and
a promissory note.

SAP is reviewed at the end of each term. A student who fails SAP is placed on financial aid warning for
one term and remains eligible; a student who still fails is suspended from aid but may appeal with
documentation of extenuating circumstances (illness, death of a relative, other special circumstances) and an
academic plan. Approved appeals result in financial aid probation for one term.
12.4 Withdrawal and Return of Funds
When a recipient of federal aid withdraws before completing 60 percent of the term, the College calculates
the portion of aid earned on a pro-rata basis and returns the unearned portion as required by regulation. The
student may owe a balance to the College as a result.
12.5 Aid Integrity
Providing false information on aid applications, or failing to report outside scholarships, may result in
cancellation of aid, repayment and referral to the appropriate authorities.

14. Health, Wellness and Accessibility
Purpose. To promote student well-being and to ensure equal access to the College's programs.
14.1 Health Requirements
All new students submit a health history and proof of immunization against measles, mumps, rubella, and
meningococcal disease (required for residents), plus tuberculosis screening for those from designated
high-risk regions. Exemptions for medical or religious reasons may be granted as the law allows;
unvaccinated students may be excluded from campus during an outbreak.
14.2 Counseling and Health Services
The Wellness Center provides primary care, counseling, psychiatric consultation, health education and
referral. Short-term counseling is free for enrolled students. Health and counseling records are confidential
and kept separate from education records; information is released only with consent or as the law requires
(for example, imminent risk of harm).
14.3 Disability Accommodations
  Students register with Accessibility Services and provide documentation of a disability.
Accommodations are decided through an interactive process and are not retroactive.
 Examples include extended time, distraction-reduced testing, alternative formats, note-taking support,
sign-language interpreters, housing and dietary adjustments.
 Faculty implement approved accommodations without needing to know a student's diagnosis.
Disagreements are resolved through Policy 21.2.
14.4 Students in Mental Health Crisis
A student who presents a risk to self or others is referred to the Behavioral Intervention Team (BIT), made
up of representatives from Student Affairs, Counseling, Campus Safety and Residence Life. The BIT may
require a risk assessment and may recommend a medical leave. A student who is on medical leave must
provide documentation from a treating professional to return.

15. Campus Safety and Security
Purpose. To protect life and property and to comply with campus safety law, including the Clery Act.
15.1 Campus Safety Department
The Department of Campus Safety operates 24 hours a day, every day. Officers patrol, respond to
emergencies, escort students, investigate incidents and work with local police, who have primary
jurisdiction over serious crimes. Emergency phones are located across campus and numbers are printed on
student IDs.
15.2 Emergency Notification and Annual Report
  The College sends emergency notifications by text, email, siren and web alerts immediately on
confirming a significant emergency or dangerous situation involving an immediate threat to the campus
community.
 Timely warnings are issued for Clery crimes that pose a continuing threat.
 The Annual Security and Fire Safety Report is published by October 1 with three years of crime
statistics, policies on reporting, prevention programs, missing-student procedures and fire safety
information.
 Students and employees must complete safety orientation and drills (fire, shelter in place, lockdown)
each year.
15.3 Alcohol, Drugs and Smoking
  Alcohol may be consumed on campus only by persons of legal age in designated private housing
spaces or at approved events with licensed service. Common-source containers, kegs and drinking
games are prohibited.
 The unlawful possession, use, manufacture or distribution of illegal drugs is prohibited on College
property or activities; Federal law applies even where state law permits cannabis, and the College
complies with the Drug-Free Schools and Communities Act.
 Misuse of prescription medication is a violation. Counseling, assessment and treatment referrals are
available.
 Smoking and vaping of any substance are prohibited inside buildings and within 25 feet of entrances;
the campus is tobacco-free outside designated areas.

15.4 Weapons
Firearms, ammunition, explosives, fireworks and other dangerous weapons are not permitted on campus, in

15.7 Campus Facilities After Hours

labs and studios requires faculty authorization. The library offers 24-hour study space during finals.

18. Student Organizations, Athletics and Events

made anonymously.

20. Employee Policies
Purpose. To set standards for faculty, staff and student employees, consistent with academic freedom and
employment law.
20.1 Academic Freedom and Responsibility
Faculty are entitled to freedom in research, publication and classroom discussion of their subjects, with the
responsibility to remain accurate, respect students and avoid presenting unrelated controversial matter. This
policy follows the principles of the 1940 Statement of Principles on Academic Freedom and Tenure.
20.2 Appointments and Evaluation
  Faculty categories: tenure-track, tenured, full-time lecturers, and adjunct instructors. Tenure-track
faculty are reviewed annually and undergo a pre-tenure review in year three and a tenure decision in
year six.
 Staff are hired through open and merit-based searches. New employees serve a 90-day introductory
period.
 All employees receive an annual performance review. Faculty teaching load is typically 4/4 for
teaching faculty and 3/3 for research-active faculty, subject to department needs.
20.3 Conflicts of Interest and Commitment
Employees must disclose, annually and when circumstances change, any financial or personal interest that
could affect, or appear to affect, their College duties. Employees may not supervise or evaluate family
members or romantic partners; consensual romantic or sexual relationships between an employee and a
student over whom the employee has academic, advising or supervisory authority are prohibited. Outside
work for pay is limited to a reasonable average of one day a week for full-time faculty and must be disclosed
and approved.
20.4 Research Integrity
Researchers must follow the highest standards of integrity and must not engage in fabrication, falsification
or plagiarism. Allegations are assessed by the Research Integrity Officer in a two-stage process (inquiry and
investigation) in line with federal regulations. Research involving human participants requires prior review
by the Institutional Review Board; research involving animals requires IACUC approval.
20.5 Workplace Conduct, Safety and Whistleblowing
  A respectful workplace free of harassment, bullying, violence and threats; weapons are prohibited.
 Good-faith reports of suspected unlawful or unethical conduct may be made to a supervisor, Human
Resources or the confidential ethics line. Retaliation is prohibited.
 Employees must report workplace injuries immediately; the College provides workers' compensation
as required by law.
